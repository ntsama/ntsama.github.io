# Teacher Training — "Write to See" Workshop  
## AI Text-to-Image Pedagogy in Priority Education Zones

**Lead Researcher:** Dr. Jean Marie Ntsama (University of Copenhagen / University of Yaoundé I)  
**Primary Supervisor:** Prof. Daniel Spikol (UCPH, Denmark)  
**Secondment Supervisor:** Prof. Marcelo Worsley (Northwestern University, USA)  
**Institutional Partner:** MINESEC — Regional Delegation of Secondary Education, Adamaoua  
**License:** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC-BY 4.0)

---

### 1. Pedagogical Objectives

By the end of the workshop, the learner will be able to:
- Produce a precise descriptive-narrative text using sensory and spatial vocabulary.
- Understand the importance of lexical precision to convey a specific intention.
- Self-revise their text by confronting their writing with an AI-generated image.

---

### 2. The Concept: "Write to See"

**Principle:** Artificial Intelligence is used not to do the work for the student, but as a **benevolent mirror**.

**Golden Rule:** If the AI can draw exactly what the student imagined, the description was clear. If the AI "misunderstands," the text lacked precision. The error is not a fault; it is data for improvement.

---

### 3. Workshop Procedure (Step-by-Step)

**Step 1 — Anchoring and Inspiration (10 min)**  
The teacher recalls the studied literary work (e.g., *Cœur du Sahel*, *Tendani Ito*, or a local folktale). Create a collective "Word Bank" on the board (e.g., mud walls, thatch, bamboo, ochre red, clay pots). Prompt: *"Describe this place or character as if you had to make someone see it who has never been to our village."*

**Step 2 — The First Draft (15 min)**  
Students individually write a short descriptive paragraph (8 to 10 lines).  
Precision Prompt: *"Use at least 3 colors, 2 geometric shapes, and describe an action or an emotion."*  
Teacher role: Circulate to unblock struggling students (vocabulary, spelling).

**Step 3 — AI Confrontation (15 min)**  
The teacher selects 3 or 4 student texts and inputs them into an AI image generator on their smartphone. Projection/Restitution: Show the generated image to the class.  
The Debate (Short Loop): *"Does the image match the author's intention? Why did the AI misunderstand?"*

**Step 4 — Revision (15 min)**  
Students revise their own texts, enriching them based on the class debate. Prompt: *"Add two precise details that the AI missed or misunderstood in the examples."*

**Step 5 — Synthesis and Valorization (5 min)**  
Read aloud 2 or 3 revised texts. Conclusion: *"Your words have the power to create images. The more precise you are, the more you are the masters of your story."*

---

### 4. Managing Heterogeneity (Inclusion)

**For struggling learners (dyslexia, attention disorders):** Do not penalize spelling mistakes in the first draft. Provide a bank of images or keywords on the board.

**For advanced/fast learners:** Ask them to add a temporal dimension (changing light) or complex emotional nuance (nostalgia, pride).

---

### 5. Simplified Evaluation Grid

| Criterion | Success Indicators | Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Lexical Precision | Use of specific vocabulary (materials, colors, spatial) | /6 |
| Spatial Structure | The place is described in an organized manner (left/right, foreground/background) | /5 |
| Narrative Coherence | Clear intention, coherent action or emotion | /5 |
| Self-Revision | Evidence of improvement after AI confrontation | /4 |

---

### 6. Practical Tips for the Teacher

- **AI is a tool, not a judge:** If the AI generates a weird image, laugh about it with the students! It shows their text must be stronger than the machine's "hallucinations."
- **Time management:** Do not waste time putting every student's text into the AI. 3 or 4 examples are enough for the whole class to understand the mechanism.
- **Ethics and Data:** Never use students' full names in the AI. Use fictional first names or initials. Do not share generated images on social media without written consent.

---

### 7. Theoretical Grounding: The Cognitive Trapeze Model

This brief is part of the Cognitive Trapeze research project and operationalizes the following concepts:
- **Short Loop (Learner–AI–Knowledge):** Immediate and tangible feedback via image generation.
- **Embodied Knowledge:** Abstract description becomes a concrete, visual, and affective experience.
- **Pedagogical Curability:** The AI does not judge; it measures, guides, and makes learning inclusive.
